Best CDL-A Jobs for Drivers With Under 2 Years of Experience

You've got your CDL-A. Maybe you've got a few months under your belt, maybe a year or so. Now you're finding out that a lot of job ads want more experience than you have. It's frustrating, but it's not a dead end. There are good jobs out there for newer drivers. You just need to know where to look and what you're trading for that first seat.

Here's a straight rundown of the kinds of CDL-A jobs that tend to open the door for drivers early in their careers.

Why Experience Matters So Much to Carriers

It mostly comes down to insurance. Carriers pay to insure every driver they put in a truck, and newer drivers usually cost more to cover. Some carriers' policies simply won't let them hire below a certain amount of experience. That's not personal. It just means you'll have better luck with carriers that are set up to hire and train newer drivers.

OTR Dry Van

Over-the-road dry van is the most common starting point. Big carriers running dry van freight across the country often have the most openings for newer drivers, and the freight is about as forgiving as it gets. No load securement like flatbed, no temperature to babysit like reefer.

The tradeoff is home time. OTR means you're out for longer stretches. For a lot of drivers it's worth it early on, because you rack up miles and experience fast, and that experience is what opens up better jobs later.

Refrigerated (Reefer)

Reefer carriers hire plenty of newer drivers too. You're hauling dry van style trailers, but you're also watching the temperature and dealing with time-sensitive loads like food. Freight tends to be steady because people eat year-round. Expect tighter appointment times and some waiting at shippers and receivers.

Dedicated Routes

Dedicated accounts mean you're hauling for one customer, often on a predictable lane. Some dedicated jobs want more experience, but not all of them. When you can land one, you usually get more consistent schedules and better home time than general OTR. Ask carriers directly whether their dedicated accounts take newer drivers.

Regional Jobs

Regional runs keep you in one part of the country and often get you home more regularly than OTR. Some carriers will hire newer drivers for regional work, especially in busy freight areas. These jobs can be harder to land with very little experience, but they're worth applying to.

Jobs That Usually Need More Time

Be realistic about a few kinds of work:

  • Tanker and hazmat. Endorsements help, but many carriers want a track record before they hand you liquid loads or hazardous materials.
  • Flatbed and heavy haul. Some flatbed carriers train newer drivers, but securement skills take time and many want experience first.
  • Local delivery with lots of tight city work. Plenty of local jobs prefer drivers who've already proven themselves backing into tough spots.

That doesn't mean don't apply. It means don't get discouraged if the first answer is "come back later."

What to Look For in Your First Carriers

When you're new, the carrier you pick shapes the rest of your career. Pay attention to more than the advertised pay.

  • Real support. Is there someone you can call when you're stuck at a dock at night? Good dispatch and driver support make a big difference early on.
  • Decent equipment. Newer drivers have enough to learn without fighting a truck that's always broken down.
  • Honest home time. Ask how often drivers actually get home, not just what the ad says.
  • Clear pay. Make sure you understand how you're paid, what counts, and what doesn't. Pay varies by carrier, so compare offers side by side.
  • Training contract terms. If the carrier paid for your schooling, read what you owe if you leave early.

Protect Your Record

Your safety record and your work history follow you. Keep your logs clean, avoid preventable accidents, and try not to bounce between carriers every few weeks. A steady, clean record is the fastest way to qualify for better-paying jobs with more home time.

If a job turns out to be a bad fit, it's okay to move on. Just do it with a plan, not out of frustration on a bad day.

Stack Up Endorsements

Endorsements like tanker, doubles and triples, and hazmat can make you more valuable down the road, even if you don't use them right away. Getting them early means you're ready when a carrier is willing to give you a shot at that freight.
